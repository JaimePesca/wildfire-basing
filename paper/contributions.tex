\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries Main Contributions}\\[0.6em]
The Cycle-Constrained Aerial Suppression Base and Water Point
Location Problem:\\ Joint Siting under Uncertainty with an Application
to Cundinamarca, Colombia\\[0.4em]
Jaime Enrique Pesca Santos and Kiana Hikaru Ysa Morla
\end{center}

\vspace{0.8em}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.7em]

\item We introduce the cycle-constrained aerial suppression base and
water point location problem, to our knowledge the first model that
sites aerial bases and water refill points jointly, with aircraft
productivity endogenous to the base to fire to water refill cycle
rather than to base-to-fire distance alone.

\item Containment is modeled physically, liters delivered against a
fire growth requirement, so the two-stage stochastic program with a
mean-risk conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) objective needs no
historical suppression outcome data, which do not exist for Colombia,
and its only nonlinearity admits an exact McCormick linearization.

\item A fix-and-optimize matheuristic whose reduced subproblems are
exact by construction solves the full 5,449-point water catalog, a
scale at which the direct model cannot even be built, with a random
destroy operator that provably escapes the local optima of purely
geographic neighborhoods.

\item The case study is built entirely from open, verified sources
around Colombia's real Firehawk acquisition, from satellite fire
detections to sample average approximation scenarios with a
statistical optimality gap of 3.4 percent, and is fully replicable
from the companion repository.

\item The computational study yields policy-relevant structure:
deployment is fleet-limited before it is physics-limited, sequential
bases-first planning matches the integrated optimum in every
certifiable regime, and the stable half of the optimal plan is a
short, nearly free list of named water points, actionable before any
aircraft arrives.

\end{enumerate}
